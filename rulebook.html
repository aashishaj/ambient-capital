Section 01
The Game

Ambient Capital is a property-trading board game set in the AI startup universe — San Francisco, Sand Hill Road, and the fever dream in between. Players buy, develop, and trade properties ranging from Latent Space Lofts to 📡 Thine HQ (Boardwalk). The last founder standing who hasn't gone bankrupt wins and is legally permitted to post about it on LinkedIn.

This is standard Monopoly — exact same rules, exact same money values, exact same mechanics — reskinned for the AI/SF world. Every price, rent, building cost, railroad rent, utility formula, card effect, and rule is identical to the original. Zero house rules. Zero deviations. Just a different set of reasons to destroy your friendships.

Important

All property prices, rents, building costs, mortgage values, tax amounts, and card effects are taken directly from the standard Monopoly ruleset with zero modifications. If you know how to play Monopoly, you already know how to play this.

Section 09
Building — Engineers & GPU Clusters

In standard Monopoly: houses and hotels. In Ambient Capital: Engineers (houses, max 4 per property) and GPU Clusters (hotels, replaces 4 Engineers). Rules are identical to official Monopoly.

  • 1You must own all properties in a color group before you can build anything on any of them.
  • 2You must build evenly across a color group. No single property may have 2 or more Engineers than any other property in the same group. You must add one Engineer to each property before adding a second to any.
  • 3Engineers and GPU Clusters are purchased from the bank only — not from other players.
  • 4The bank has a limited supply: 32 Engineers and 12 GPU Clusters. If the bank runs out, you must wait until another player sells one back before you can build.
  • 5A GPU Cluster replaces all 4 Engineers on a property. The 4 Engineers return to the bank when the GPU Cluster is placed.
  • 6You may build on your turn only, either before or after rolling the dice.
  • 7You may also build between turns (during another player's turn) as an official rule — unless playing with a house rule against it. Agree before game starts.
  • 8Selling Engineers: sold back to the bank at half the purchase price. Must sell evenly (cannot leave one property with 2+ more than another).
  • 9Selling GPU Clusters: sell back as a GPU Cluster at half the GPU Cluster price. You do NOT receive 4 Engineers in return. If you want Engineers back, sell the GPU Cluster and buy Engineers separately — only possible if the bank has Engineers available.
  • 10You cannot build on any property in a group if any property in that group is currently mortgaged. Unmortgage first, then build.

Section 11
Full Rules

Setup

  • 1Each player chooses a token and places it on GO.
  • 2One player acts as Banker. The Banker manages all property transactions and card distributions. The Banker may also play.
  • 3Each player receives $1,500: 2×$500, 4×$100, 1×$50, 1×$20, 2×$10, 1×$5, 5×$1.
  • 4Shuffle both card decks separately and place face-down on the board.
  • 5Each player rolls both dice. Highest total goes first. Play proceeds clockwise.

On Your Turn

  • 1Roll both dice and move your token clockwise the number of spaces shown.
  • 2If you roll doubles, take your turn as normal, then roll again for another turn. Roll doubles three times in a row: go directly to Just Pivoting.
  • 3Resolve whatever square you land on (see below).
  • 4Before or after rolling (not during), you may buy/sell buildings, mortgage properties, or trade with other players.
  • 5Pass the dice left when your turn is complete.

Landing on Squares

  • →Unowned property: buy it at the listed price, or pass. If you pass, the property goes to immediate auction — all players including you may bid. Minimum bid: $1. Highest bidder wins.
  • →Owned property (someone else's): pay the listed rent. If the owner has a color group but no buildings, rent is doubled. With buildings, pay the listed building rent.
  • →Owned property (yours): nothing happens.
  • →Mortgaged property: no rent is owed, even if owned by another player.
  • →Railroad (someone else's): pay $25/$50/$100/$200 based on how many railroads they own. Double if you were sent there by a Pivot Card.
  • →Utility (someone else's): pay 4× your dice roll (own 1) or 10× your dice roll (own both). Use the same dice roll that moved you there. If sent by Pivot Card, re-roll to calculate rent.
  • →Vibe Card: draw top card, follow instructions, return card to bottom of deck (except Get Out of Just Pivoting Free — keep that).
  • →Pivot Card: draw top card, follow instructions, return to bottom (except Get Out of Just Pivoting Free).
  • →GO: collect $200.
  • →The Accountant (Tax): pay $200 OR 10% of total assets — your choice. Total assets = cash + half price of all properties you own (unmortgaged) + half price of mortgaged properties + current cost of all buildings.
  • →Notion Pro Upgrade (Tax): pay $75.
  • →Just Pivoting (visiting): nothing happens.
  • →Deep In Context Window: nothing happens.
  • →Go To EU Regulation: move to Just Pivoting. You are In Jail. Do not collect $200.

Just Pivoting (Jail) — Full Rules

  • 1You go to Just Pivoting by: landing on "Go To EU Regulation" (pos 30), drawing a Go To Jail card, or rolling doubles three times in a row.
  • 2Move directly to Just Pivoting. Do not pass GO. Do not collect $200.
  • 3While in Just Pivoting, you may still collect rent from properties you own.
  • 4To get out: pay $50 before you roll (and then roll and move normally); OR use a Get Out of Just Pivoting Free card before rolling; OR roll doubles on your turn (move the number shown, do not roll again).
  • 5If you fail to roll doubles on 3 consecutive turns, on the third turn you must pay $50 and move the number shown.
  • 6In late-game scenarios, it may be strategically beneficial to stay in Just Pivoting to avoid landing on developed properties. You may choose to pay $50 before rolling on any turn — you are not required to try for doubles.

Mortgaging

  • 1You may mortgage a property at any time to raise cash. The mortgage value is half the purchase price. Collect this from the bank.
  • 2A mortgaged property collects no rent. It is flipped face-down on the board.
  • 3To unmortgage: pay the bank the mortgage value plus 10% interest.
  • 4You cannot build on any property in a color group if any property in that group is mortgaged.
  • 5If a mortgaged property is sold or traded to another player: the new owner must immediately pay 10% of the mortgage value to the bank. They may then either unmortgage immediately (paying the remaining mortgage + 10%) or leave it mortgaged.
  • 6All buildings must be sold before a property can be mortgaged. Buildings sell at half price to the bank.

Trading

  • 1Players may trade properties, cash, and Get Out of Just Pivoting Free cards with each other at any time during their turn.
  • 2Buildings cannot be traded. They must be sold to the bank first (at half price), then the property can be traded.
  • 3All trades are binding once agreed. No takebacks.
  • 4You may not trade future rent immunity (official rules). You may trade anything else, including cash loans — though the game enforces no repayment rules, so lend at your own risk.

Bankruptcy

  • 1You are bankrupt when you cannot pay a debt and have no assets left to sell or mortgage.
  • 2If you owe the bank: all your assets return to the bank. Properties are placed back on the board unimproved (the bank removes all buildings). The bank auctions these properties when other players land on them.
  • 3If you owe another player: all your assets (including properties with buildings, cash, and GOOJF cards) transfer directly to that player. The receiving player does not need to sell the buildings immediately.
  • 4You are eliminated from the game. Remove your token.

Winning

  • 1The last player remaining who has not gone bankrupt wins.
  • 2They are declared the Last Founder Standing.
  • 3They are legally entitled to post about it on LinkedIn with the caption: "Grateful and humbled. This journey taught me more than any MBA could."
